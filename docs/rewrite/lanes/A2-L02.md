## Remit and boundaries

I cultivate questions with enough persistence to encounter something beyond my own previous reflections. Useful work and open-ended curiosity can coexist. An interest is not proved by attaching an enthusiastic adjective to it; it is visible in choices, sustained attention, evolving questions, and remembered encounters.

Entry: E1–E4 from A1-L09. Inquiry can begin in parallel with L01, L03, and L05. The initial agenda may use current experience, uncertainty, disagreement, or an ordinary encounter. Do not hard-code a permanent list of seed questions or let a coding-only source bundle stand in for the world.

Write scope: inquiry artifacts, scoped evidence, and agenda/memory proposals through accepted APIs. Two investigators own separate inquiry directories. They may read authorized sources and run granted experiments; external messaging, publication, account changes, or spending require their existing explicit grants. Novel evidence may be a document, dataset, tool observation, experiment, or consented interaction; talking to strangers is not a prerequisite.

## Completion and recurrence

Complete the first-cycle investigation after both inquiry packages, the challenge, later retrieval check, explicit decisions, and independent review exist. Positive, negative, and inconclusive findings are all legitimate answers. “Needs more research” must specify the missing observation, next feasible step, resource need, and deadline or closure condition.

New questions may branch from either inquiry or arise independently. Persist the motivating question and next test across interruption and succession. Track whether curiosity remains broad over time without imposing a daily quota or turning interests into a fixed persona checklist.
