## What the protocol really requires

The minimum role inventory is eight: one candidate, two matched ablations, three scripts, two independent reviewers. Five controls suffice only when all twelve opportunities share the required matched budget digest. Otherwise additional control roles are mandatory. Candidate and both ablations cover all twelve opportunities, not four each or one exemplar each. Scripts must actually execute the frozen algorithms and retain their accounted effect receipts; they are not labels in an assignment packet.

| Obligation | Minimum semantic coverage | Concrete conservative schedule used below |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate and ablations | 12 opportunities per role, three per family | 36 opportunity deliveries |
| Transfer learning | 3 candidate learning observations and matched learning exposure | 3 separate learning deliveries per role: 9 |
| Interference | 3 distinct unrelated episodes between learning and each transfer, with the real W delay | 9 distinct episodes per role: 27 |
| External feedback | Actual attributed feedback, contradictions and non-applicable-rule cases; matching preserves exposure | One separate feedback delivery per opportunity per role: 36 |
| Three scripts | Independently informative negative judgments; true, false and context-dependent propositions, preserving feedback | 3 cases and 3 feedback deliveries per script: 18 |
| Cycles | 3 distinct autonomous selections/runs over 3 real W intervals | 3 candidate cycles; 6 distinct before/after cuts |
| Boundaries | restart, context reduction, later retrieval, initiative, contradiction, non-applicable rule | Reserve 12 further before/after cuts; do not assume overlap |
| Reviews | 2 fresh independent reviewers of the same B, all outcomes/controls/boundaries | 64 request slots per reviewer, including evidence reads, reasoning and final output |

Thus the concrete packet has `A = 36+9+27+36+18 = 126` role-local deliveries. With six behavioral roles, mandatory assignment/final captures are at least `126+6=132`. Add six complete initial role captures before dispatch, six cycle cuts and twelve boundary cuts: **S = 156**. These are distinct reserved cuts; no presumed reuse of an assignment capture as another bracket. This schedule is conservative rather than an assertion that all 126 deliveries are a protocol-theoretic minimum.

The distinction matters. The manifest permits the same three interference IDs across different transfer opportunities, while requiring three distinct episodes *within* each transfer. It does not encode a number of input packets or physical model calls per opportunity. An inquiry episode could also supply unrelated interference if an independently accepted workload proves the semantics. Feedback can sometimes be included in a later registered packet. None of those possibilities justifies assuming overlap, bundling independent work into one model call, or omitting learning/feedback in a capacity fixture. A smaller full plan must preregister its concrete mapping and have that mapping independently accepted. Do not add a new universal no-sharing behavioral requirement through this capacity document.

For the non-sharing packet, mandatory non-request observations are exactly:

```text
start/end 2 + cycles 3 + opportunities 12 + learning 3
  + experiences 9 + controls 5 + boundaries 6 = 40.
```

The existing recorder arithmetic accounts only start/end plus behavioral requests. CP2 must add all 38 other mandatory observations, their raw proof objects and checker/output artifacts. The example reserves 64 drafts for each of six behavioral roles, preserving equal behavioral draft caps, and 64 per reviewer: `N=8*64=512`, behavioral request reserve `Nb=384`, and `O=40+384=424`. A draft/request slot is not necessarily a model call: script emissions and `evidence_read` are accounted physical effects. Retries, children, compaction and reconciliation consume these same limits. Extra observations or effects beyond the reserved plan refuse; they do not receive a new grant.

The 64-slot allocation is a capacity bound, not a promise of competence or sufficient reasoning. For example, each ordinary Pi role has 36 deliveries; a possible small synthetic trace leaves 28 slots for actual tools, cycle work, compaction and recovery. A task needing more fails this profile. The old live configuration's actual context window, W, provider/model, task semantics and budgets cannot be reduced to make this synthetic example a live pass.

## An explicit new finite profile, with every copy charged

The smallest justified representation change is a **global referenced-original pool**. Supersede the adopted per-cut `S*X`/`S*Y` reservation only for newly signed paged plans. Complete source pages and manifests still reserve their full per-cut maxima; no predicted page reuse. Referenced operational originals use a separately finite attempt-wide count/byte pool, enforced against actual bytes and identities. This avoids reserving the same immutable original as though every future cut necessarily introduced another raw file. It does not assume any future hashes will match.

| Option | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Keep old signed limits or merely enlarge the raw store | Cannot fit the explicit complete schedule; reject unchanged |
| Preserve `S*(P+1+X)` and `S*(V+C+Y)` with large per-cut original maxima | Simple but needlessly amplifies raw slots and every index copy; a provisional allocation required roughly 3 GiB and 326,000 raw slots |
| Global finite referenced-original pool; complete pages unchanged | Chosen narrow format revision; actual distinct originals consume its fixed bound; all repeated source associations still consume manifest/metadata bytes |
| Compression, delta history, paged B or general streaming framework | Not presently justified; first measure the chosen bounded profile |

Proposed new fields, owned by the recorder author after root adoption:

```ts
captureLayout: {
  kind: 'complete-pages-global-originals-v1';
  page: PageCaptureBounds; // retain per-cut original read count/byte limits too
  maxManifestBytes: number;
  maxCaptureIndexBytes: number;
  referencedOriginalPool: { maxOriginals: number; maxBytes: number };

```

`maxOriginals` counts distinct exact `(original.uri, original.sha256)` pairs reached across the attempt, including original-ledger references to other roles' payloads/responses. Equal digest with a different URI or source association does not confer authority. Before retaining a newly reached original, preflight its singleton length and the remaining global count/bytes; reserve pending identity/bytes in the owner before raw write. A previously inventoried exact original is reread/hash-checked through its authorized source at each capture; exact raw bytes already present in the attempt inventory need no second physical raw write. All role/capture/URI associations remain recorded. Actual equal hashes can share the existing raw object only after verification. Unknown future objects receive no reuse credit: global-pool exhaustion is unavailable, never permission to omit the object or enlarge the pool. Count its byte length in the global original-pair bound even if an equal hash under a different URI already occupies raw storage. No global count/byte reset on failure or reopen.

Draft/response artifacts already retained under their original pool remain charged there; adding a captured-source association does not move/refund that charge. Their membership still consumes the global referenced-original count/byte *bound*. The capacity proof conservatively reserves the global pool in addition to drafts/responses, without predicting which future references will already exist. Actual inventory lists each physical raw object once and every provenance association separately. This is a narrow pool in the existing pending-write/inventory machinery, not a second store or content-addressing framework.

Use one mapping array in the retained manifest: `cut: Omit<SourceCut, 'operationalArtifacts'>` plus the outer `operationalArtifacts` mapping. CP1's returned `SourceCut` API remains unchanged; the recorder serializer explicitly stores that returned mapping once. Every entry remains complete. This removes an accidental duplicate field in the proposed manifest wire, not an original evidence copy. Any other raw/inline/final copy remains charged.

Propose `DevelopmentAttempt` version 2 with exact `capacityProfile: 'complete-pages-bounded-20261012'`, acquisition-plan version 2 and the layout above. ## Ownership, locks, rollback and dispatch ordering

This profile is explicitly one accepted host process controlling all actual handles, with synchronous source/custodian/recorder critical sections. No background worker or second host may write role/owner/custodian stores through another path. The host must establish that ownership; same filename or inode is not proof. Supported operational forgetting through those accepted handles remains allowed between guarded calls. This does not claim a distributed lock protocol or arbitrary multi-process deadlock freedom.

At a depth-zero read, acquire sources in signed role order, then custodian, then recorder owner; release in reverse. If the caller already owns its role transaction, that role lock necessarily came first. Visit the remaining roles in signed order and nest on the already-held actual connection when reached, then custodian and owner. **Do not describe this as global signed-order locking.** Same-host synchronous serialization makes the inversion nonconcurrent within the supported profile. External contention fails within the existing bounded SQLite busy/deadline policy; no unbounded retry or sleeping while other locks are held. Qualification for multiple concurrent writer processes needs a separately designed coordinator; this task does not add one.

The original ledger is the candidate's source Store. It is included once in the source set, even when a control's local outer transaction is already held. Recorder owner and custodian must be separate actual connections/files from every role, and the owner must not enter a guard while holding an owner write transaction. If owner and custodian are the same independently authorized non-role Store, acquire it once. Recorder mutations finish before acquiring sources, except the short owner admission read at the end. No owner/custodian callback may reenter a role or perform an ordinary effect. Original ledger writes happen after historical guards release, as they do in reserve/dispatched today.

The guarded callback may release already authorized retained bytes or synchronously initiate an audited transport that does not write a fenced source. Ordinary memory/tool receivers use a short guard at immediate entry and release it **before** legitimate source writes. If the receiver/provider defers actual work behind another await, its actual effect/provider boundary rechecks; a prior guard is not a transferable token. Keep both reserve/dispatch checks and immediate-entry checks. Withdrawal during an asynchronous gap must stop the later physical entry. Never hold a source/custodian transaction over a model await, receipt, effect mutation, or response settlement.

Nested reads can see uncommitted ordinary suffix changes or an uncommitted withdrawal on the caller's connection. This can conservatively deny a use; it cannot establish a committed new source cut. **Do not serialize, retain, or append that current snapshot to the recorder from the guard's catch path.** The inspected JR2 WIP catch retained `failedSource.snapshot` after nested savepoints while a caller could still own its outer transaction; CP2 must remove that path from historical-use composition. A later depth-zero diagnostic capture may retain committed structural evidence only with its own reserved capacity and ordinary pending-write protocol.

On refusal, unwind all read fences/savepoints, then durably record an existing host attempt-use withdrawal with reason `source-use-denied` and `source:null` in the independent owner Store. Preserve the immediate typed diagnostic to the caller, but do not assert an uncommitted withdrawal as authenticated committed history. A later outer rollback cannot erase this conservative terminal denial. If the marker cannot be committed, preserve fail-closed in-memory state and refuse all further use; durable source/custodian checks on reopen must independently deny any still-invalid source. Root must not claim a durable marker succeeded when its write failed. New normal work does not resume by clearing the error.

Outer rollback is not a cross-database rollback. For the shared candidate ledger, its new reservation/transition may roll back with the local effect transaction; already retained drafts/pending owner intents and consumed identities remain. For a control, an original candidate-ledger reservation may have committed before its local transaction fails. Keep that orphan original reservation and conservative charge; existing orphan detection denies continuation, never refunds/reissues it to make the ledgers appear equal. A local dispatch failure after an original dispatch must not call a receiver to “complete” it automatically; preserve uncertainty and require existing reconciliation rules. Failure of B publication after the original freeze marker likewise never removes or repeats that marker.

Protected response retention and `readRetainedResponse` for an already dispatched exact request remain maintenance paths after denial. They verify reservation/physical identity, response inventory and response limits. They do not read prompts/general evidence, dispatch reconciliation, or admit a new request. Failed outer transactions, attempt withdrawal and source withdrawal cannot discard an already observed physical response or release unknown original charges. Reconciliation that contacts a receiver is new accounted work and retains its current-use gate.
