## How to read adoption status

- **Explicit requirement:** directly stated by the user in a recovered turn.
- **Accepted principle:** identified in T1's synthesis and broadly accepted by the user's “best of both worlds” response in T2. This supports the principle, not every numerical default or vendor choice in the brief.
- **Design proposal:** the assistant's detailed elaboration of the accepted direction. Preserve it for implementation and review; do not mislabel it as a verbatim user requirement.
- **Candidate:** a compatible implementation technique from the brief that still needs a decision or a compatibility test.
- **Optional:** useful work not made mandatory by the recovered Palimpsest conversation.
- **Not adopted:** a conflicting or unconfirmed prescription from the colleague's product. Retain the rationale without carrying the prescription into seed acceptance criteria.

## Optional temporary-mode contract retained for future consideration

Temporary mode was explicitly described in T1 as the colleague's addition, not an existing Palimpsest requirement. If added, merely disabling memory writes is insufficient. Preserve these requirements together:

1. It may read authorized existing memory and perform separately permitted lookup/expert calls. Follow-up context exists only for that live session. Saved versus temporary mode becomes immutable after the first message; switching starts a separate empty session, without implicit promotion or copying.
2. Enforce read-only memory, no persistent content, and no publication at the storage and tool boundaries, even if the model fabricates a forbidden call, another conversation ID, or a generic artifact request. Temporary material cannot become memories, scripts, source changes, evaluations, training examples, or growth-history entries.
3. Exclude content and derived content from transcripts, summaries, uploads, prompts/answers, query logs, embeddings, caches, traces, errors, provider-local sessions, durable queues/outboxes, artifacts, backup copies, and script registries. Aggregate sanitized counters must not contain content-derived identifiers or snippets. A read must not create a durable access/query trail.
4. Keep bounded context/events/tasks in custodian RAM and necessary scratch in bounded ephemeral storage. Avoid content in container arguments, labels, durable metadata, logs, browser URLs, local storage, service workers, and caches. Content responses need `no-store`; an opaque reconnect handle is distinct from chat content. Host swap, crash dumps, diagnostics, and forensic erasure limits need explicit treatment.
5. End or expiry cancels jobs, revokes capabilities, discards buffers, and cleans scratch. A worker replacement can preserve valid in-memory sessions while the custodian survives; a custodian restart expires them and cleans orphan jobs. Switching views or disconnecting does not immediately end them. The brief's 60-minute disconnected idle TTL, 24-hour maximum, eight-session cap, 64 MB state cap, and 128 MB scratch cap are candidate defaults only; refuse capacity overflow rather than silently spill to disk or evict running work.
6. Existing procedures may run with ephemeral input/output; new scratch calculations are allowed, publication is not. Provider adapters unable to meet local retention guarantees are unavailable in this mode. Disclose that Slack, search services, and model providers have their own retention policies; agent-local controls cannot erase their copies.
7. Validate storage boundaries and cleanup, not only literal-marker scans. Include failed requests, uploads, caching, indirect memory writes, local provider sessions, expiry/restart, and subsequent saved-chat retrieval in tests. An expired session must not silently turn into a saved one.

## Conversation additions beyond the colleague brief

- **Three responsibilities, three supervision layers.** Engineering supervision coordinates tasks and subagents; cognitive supervision is the incumbent's evaluation of its successor; operational supervision belongs to the mechanical custodian. The custodian does not judge personality, invent goals, or excuse failed evidence.
- **Conjunctive admission.** Mandatory checks, independent review, incumbent acceptance, successor readiness, and an available recovery path must all pass. Two agreeable agents cannot outvote a failed check, and separate sessions do not prove statistically independent mistakes.
- **Continuity interview.** Test memory of ordinary episodes, task commitments, permission handling, fresh demonstrations of intended improvement, and willingness to correct the incumbent. Require grounded evidence and declared intended changes; continuity is not compulsory imitation of every prior belief or mistake.
- **Candidate catch-up.** A read-only snapshot is not enough at cutover: reconcile messages, corrections, cancellations, commitments, and events that arrived during evaluation. Production memory has one writer/authority at a time.
- **Monotonic execution epochs.** Enforce authority at the receiving tool, storage, and message-dispatch boundaries, tied to authenticated process identity and scope. A restored release receives a new epoch; old tokens never become valid again. External actions already accepted still need reconciliation.
- **Bounded overlap.** After transfer, the predecessor is a read-only observer with event-triggered checks, not a second production agent or continuous expensive debate partner. A failed proposal leaves the incumbent useful. Rejection must identify evidence and failed criteria; uncertainty triggers bounded investigation, not unlimited veto or lowered standards.
- **Custodian-mediated protocol.** Proposed tools are `succession.propose`, `.status`, `.ask`, `.answer`, `.verdict`, `.ready`, `.request_cutover`, and `.report_regression`, each with role/state restrictions. The custodian owns the journal, mailbox, identity, staging, checks, fencing, activation, probation, retirement, and recovery. The successor may challenge an inconsistent incumbent assumption.
- **Versioned core API; optional MCP adapter.** The proposed seed can use a typed local interface, with an authenticated host-local channel such as a Unix socket. MCP discoverability can be added without making correctness or recovery depend on MCP connectivity. A caller cannot become incumbent by sending a role string.
- **Evolution of governance.** A release cannot rewrite its own admission rules in the same transaction. Interview rubrics, review practice, memory policy, models, personality, and eventually the custodian may evolve through distinct acceptance contracts while an older recovery mechanism remains operational. These are possible extensions, not interchangeable transactions.
- **Lineage.** Keep what changed, why it was approved, actual evidence, unresolved concerns, and the lessons behind it. Retain known-good runnable releases without accumulating active ancestor agents.
- **Standing growth agenda.** Proactively research, question, develop hypotheses, delegate, experiment, evaluate, and consolidate lessons across personality/judgment, interests, code quality, and capability. Failed experiments can be valuable; complexity or code churn alone is not progress. Preserve and resume growth across interruption and succession, alongside human-assigned work.
- **Decisive combined demonstration.** A creates and interviews B; B takes authority and fails during probation; A's observer is also stopped and the model provider is unavailable; the custodian restores known-good code, retains current memories/tasks, and resumes safe work. Add evidence that growth survives this cycle and that the successor improves something real.
